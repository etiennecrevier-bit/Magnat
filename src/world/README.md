The world draws the game state; it never changes it. It reads `GameState` and the last day's visits, and reports player clicks to the UI layer, which calls the engine.

- It may import the types, the engine, finance and content functions that only read, and the UI store (`useGame`, `gameStore.getState()`) to read the game. It never imports the functions that change the game (`postEntry`, `applyDecision`, `tick`, `playDay`, and the others `eslint.config.js` lists) and never calls the store's `advance` or `setState`. Its tests may, to build their fixtures.
- Engine, finance and content never import anything from here.
- Randomness and the browser are allowed here, since nothing the world does reaches the engine: a customer's walk can wobble without changing the game.
- `WorldCanvas.tsx` mounts the Pixi application in a fixed layer behind the React panels, sized to the window. While it is on the page, `WorldCanvas.css` puts the panels (`main`) on an opaque cream card with an ink outline, so no scenery runs behind their text; on a phone the card spans the window.

## The street

`WorldCanvas.tsx` starts the street with `startStreet` (`street.ts`), while « Ville » is closed (see « The city »), which loads the title font and the art, builds the scene (`scene.ts`), fits it to the window (`layout.ts`) and redraws it each time the store's game changes.

- **The manifest** is `src/content/assets.json`: every piece's id, layer, position and size in the 1440 × 900 design, its colour, and optionally an image. Layers are drawn back to front in the order listed, and pieces in each layer in the order listed. Without an image, a piece is drawn as its kind's placeholder from `placeholders.ts`: simple shapes in the palette's colours, without outlines, lit as « The light » says. The trees and the lamp post stand in the `furniture` layer, over the `shadows` layer, which holds no piece.
- **The backdrop** (#198) is art, not code: the sky, the clouds, Mont Royal and the far towers (`far`), the five triplexes, the dépanneur, the sidewalk, the curb, the road, the lamp post and the two trees are SVG files under `art/street/`, cut from the design reference's `street-target.svg` (`docs/design/reference/`, #197) with their own light and shade. Each is a `backdrop` piece at the reference's coordinates, in its paint order. Wide windows show past the design's edges, so the row goes on there in the manifest: the first triplex mirrored (`triplex-1-mirrored.svg`, its windows and door swapped, still lit from the left) and a copy of the fifth on the left, two more copies of the fifth on the right; the sidewalk, the curb and the road are tiles one design wide, three side by side; the sky is the reference's gradient stretched, its top colour above the design for tall phones. The reference's grain, vignette and warm glow are left out, since `atmosphere.ts` adds them over the whole world, and so are its people, which stay in code. An SVG is drawn once, when it loads, at the manifest's `artResolution` (2) pixels per logical pixel of its own size, so it stays sharp at a window scale times the screen's density of up to 2; no file is more than 4096 pixels a side at that resolution, which is why the clouds, the ground and the sky are their own files rather than one wide picture. « DÉPANNEUR » is written in Fredoka embedded in its file, since an image cannot reach the page's fonts: the game ships weight 600, thickened by a thin ink stroke to the reference's 700. A backdrop whose image fails to load is a flat, unlit shape in its colour, so the street keeps its layout. The « MENU » chalkboard (#208), an A-frame on the sidewalk right of the truck, comes from concept B of the reference (`ui_concepts.py`) instead, with « MENU » in embedded Fredoka; it shows on open days only and is listed with the scenery, before what the events put on the street, so the « Rue barrée » sign stands in front of it. `art/cut.py` cuts the files again from the reference (`python3 cut.py <reference folder>`), the street's and the truck's, and checks that the pieces' lines, in order, are the reference's; nothing in the build or the tests runs it.
- **The truck** (#199) is art too, cut from the same reference by `art/cut.py` into `art/truck/`, at the reference's place: the body with its cab, wheels and warm interior (`truck.svg`), the espresso machine, the croissant rack, the croissants, the counter, the awning and the sign's board, drawn in the reference's paint order. Its wheels stand on the top of the road (y 716), and its shadow on the road (`truck-shadow`) is a backdrop in the `street` layer, under the people's. The counter's image is the strip of the truck in front of the avatar: the counter with the body and the interior behind it, cut from the truck's own lines, so it lines up with the truck and hides the avatar's legs. The rack's glare is drawn behind the croissants, so it stays when the rack is empty, and the croissants are the reference's on three shelves, one a step of `fill`. The owner (the avatar), the business name, « CAFÉ · CROISSANTS » (`lettering`, written in Fredoka in its colour, as tall as its box), the steam, the chocolate pot and what shows on some days only (the rolled awning, « Fermé », « Hors service ») stay in code, over or under the art as the reference layers them. The rival truck parks at our curb at our scale.
- **Real art** replaces a placeholder without code, and is never lit or shadowed again by code: put the file under `src/world/art/` (PNG, JPEG, WebP, AVIF or SVG) and set the piece's `image` to its path there, such as `"image": "truck.png"` or `"image": "truck/body.png"`. `art.ts` collects every file in that folder when Vite builds, and the image is stretched to the piece's size. A missing file or one that fails to load warns in the console and keeps the placeholder. `art/dummy.png` is a test image that proves the path from the folder to the screen; no piece uses it.
- **Open or closed** follows the day in the top bar (`game.day`), as `tick()` will play it: with that day's decisions, not those waiting for the next morning, and closed on a day a change to the street closes the truck (see « Events on the street »). A piece with `"shows": "open"` (the awning, the steam) or `"shows": "closed"` (the rolled awning, the « Fermé » sign) only shows on those days.
- **The croissant rack** shows the croissants the truck opens with that morning: those on hand plus the morning restock, or only those on hand on a closed day. Croissants keep one day, so none are on hand between days. `fill.capacity` croissants fill the rack, shown in `fill.steps` shelves; any croissant shows a shelf (`rackFill` in `truckView.ts`). The restock rule repeats the engine's, which the world may not call; `truckView.test.ts` holds the two together.
- **The sign** reads the business name from the player's profile (`game.profile.businessName`, « Le P'tit Camion » unless the player picks another) in Fredoka SemiBold (`fonts/`, SIL Open Font License), shrunk to fit.
- **Scaling** keeps the design's proportions: as large as the window allows, centred across and standing on the bottom edge. A taller window shows more sky; a wider one shows more of the row of triplexes, the sidewalk and the road, which run past the design's edges.

## Events on the street

The event cards' changes to the street show on it, as placeholders in the look of « The light » until real art exists. Every piece, its place and what it says are in `assets.json`; the world only reads the game.

- **Which day**: like the truck, the street shows the day in the top bar (`game.day`), as `tick()` will play it. `modifiersToday` (`truckView.ts`) gives the changes in effect that day, as `playDay` will sum them up in `lastDay.modifiers`: those the choices made already, then those a choice queued for that morning (the festival's Friday, « Fermé demain », the chalet's Saturday). So the cones go up the morning the player chooses, not a day late. The rule repeats the engine's start of the day, which the world may not call; `truckView.test.ts` holds the two together over a summer of a seeded game where every card that changes the street comes up.
- **The pieces**: a piece with `"shows": { "event": "<card>" }` shows while any change from that card is in effect, and one with `"shows": { "unavailable": "<product>" }` while that product cannot be sold (`showsOn`). An `"except"` list of the card's choices leaves out the changes they made: `chosenModifiers` (`truckView.ts`) finds the choice behind each change in `events.history`, the last of its card on its first day or before; a change a Défi forces has none and always shows. Construction (`conesOrange`) puts rows of orange cones and a « Rue barrée » sign on the edge of the sidewalk, except after « Changer de coin de rue » (`changerDeCoin`): the truck has left them, though its new corner keeps fewer walk-ins; a competitor (`concurrence`) parks a green truck behind ours at the right edge, its board « La Patate Pressée » beside our sign; a festival hangs bunting across the street and a « Festival du Pas Pire » banner; café unavailable tapes a « Hors service » card to the espresso machine; a heat wave (`canicule`) warms the sky. The shadows of the cones, the sign's legs and the rival truck show and hide with them.
- **The fans**: while the crowd's `fans` show (`assets.json`, read as `fans` in `crowdSettings.ts`), each customer who comes fans themselves with its `chance`: a folding fan in the hand on the right, swinging in real time and frozen on pause. Those who came before keep their hands free, and once the heat wave ends every fan on the street goes at once, before the next frame, paused or not.
- **Closed by an event**: a day a change closes the truck (`opensToday`) looks like a day off: the awning rolled up, « Fermé », no avatar, nothing replayed, and the rack shows only the croissants on hand, since the engine skips the restock. While a product cannot be sold, the rack likewise shows no restock of it.

## The light

The look approved for #89: one sun from the upper left, every shadow to the lower right. Every number is in `light` in `assets.json`.

- **Three tones** (`light.ts`): a face looking up is its colour mixed with the sun (`#FFF1CF`), with a brighter rim along its front edge; a face looking at the viewer keeps its colour; a face turned away is mixed with slate (`#34404F`), never black. Colours mix channel by channel in 8-bit sRGB, rounding half to even, which is how the rule sheet's values come out (brick `#B4532F` lit from above is `#C47652`). A facade is graded from light at the top to slate at the foot.
- **The placeholders** (`placeholders.ts`) draw each surface in its tones: the truck's roof and the counter's top as top faces, the cabin's nose and the right of each post in shade, and a short slate shadow fading down under the counter's top and the awning. Their textures (`materials.ts`, from `noise.ts`) are made once when the street starts and freed when it stops. The backdrop's and the truck's art come with their own light and shade and are never lit by code.
- **The shadows on the ground** (`shadows.ts`) go in the manifest's shadow layer: the truck's placeholder, when its art fails to load, the event pieces standing on the street and each customer and child cast a slate shape to the lower right, with a soft ellipse under each foot, wheel and post. The cast shadows are drawn opaque, then blurred and faded together by one filter, so where two overlap the ground is no darker. A person's shadows are the design reference's (`person()` in `rue.py`, #197): a soft slate spot under the feet at 45 % and a longer, flatter one to the lower right at 20 %, their sizes and offsets shares of the person's height in `figure.shadow` (`crowdSettings.ts`), the same whichever way they face. The long ones go in a group of cast shadows of their own (`castOval`), drawn opaque and faded together at their opacity like the others, so two people's overlap is no darker either; the spot is a contact shadow (`spot`), whose offset sits in its anchor. Pixi's Canvas 2D renderer, used where WebGL and WebGPU are missing, has no filters: there the cast shadows are drawn opaque into one texture (`cacheAsTexture`), redrawn each frame at the screen's pixels, which shows at their opacity. Overlaps stay no darker there too; only the soft edge is missing. The people's long shadows get a texture of their own the same way.
- **People** (`people.ts`) are lit on the screen's left whichever way they face: each customer is drawn twice, to face right and to face left, and the figure shows the one that matches. Nothing outlines them: the arm, the hand and the leg away from the sun are mixed with slate, the arm toward it with a little sun, and the torso, a coat and the head take the reference's gradients from sun to slate (`faceH` and `roundLit`), textures made once per side when the street starts (`createPeopleShading`) and stretched over each shape. What someone carries keeps its side as they turn. Their shadows stay on the ground as they step, each under its own feet, a child's included.
- **Over the world** (`atmosphere.ts`), never over the panels: the sun's warm glow from the upper left corner, the painted unevenness in soft light, the vignette and the film grain in overlay, each from seeded noise, made once. Pixi's overlay and soft light blend a sprite's colour after its alpha has darkened it, so these sprites stay opaque and their textures hold the opacity (`strayOf`). The blends need `pixi.js/advanced-blend-modes` and WebGL's back buffer, which the street turns on for them. Pixi starts without it (`worldOptions` in `mountWorld.ts`), since only then does it antialias the canvas itself; the back buffer antialiases as well, so the edges stay smooth with either visual effects. The world's canvas sits in the fixed layer under the page, so all of it stays under the panels; the tally, which shows numbers, goes over the atmosphere, in the stage's `labels`.
- **« Effets visuels »**: « Réglages » sets them (`src/ui/settings.ts`), and the street follows the change at once. « Simples » drops the grain, the unevenness and the blur of the shadows, and turns WebGL's back buffer off; nothing else changes.

## The customers

`street.ts` starts the customers with `startCustomers` (`customers.ts`) and moves them on each frame at the store's speed. Each concern has its own module, and the rules are tested without Pixi.

- **What replays** (`replay.ts`): during each day, the street replays the last day played, `game.lastDay`. Its visits come up in arrival order as the world's own clock goes through the day: a day lasts `msPerDayAtNormalSpeed` at ×1, and a visit comes up once the day is as far through as its arrival is through the opening hours. The clock starts again on a new day or a new game, not when a decision changes the game. Nothing replays on a day the truck is closed: the avatar is gone, and everyone still waiting walks off without buying and without a bubble. The open day after a closed one has no visits to replay, so its street stays empty.
- **The sampling** is visual only and never changes a number. Each visit that comes up is kept with the speed's `keepChance`, so busier days and busier hours bring more people. A kept visit walks on only if the speed's gap (`minGapMs`, give or take `jitterMs`) has gone by since the last one and fewer than its `maxOnScreen` are on screen; otherwise it is dropped, never held back, so those who walk on come when they came. When the next day starts, the visits still to come are dropped. A day lasts 4 s at ×1 but a walk about 10 s, so the crowd is a stream running across days rather than one day played out.
- **The cap** gates who comes, at each speed: ×1 lets in up to 10, ×3 up to 5. Going from ×1 to ×3 takes no one off the street: those there finish their visit, faster, and no one new comes until fewer than 5 are left. At ×10, no one walks: the street clears and a badge counts the day replayed, « 87 clients servis »: the visits that reached the counter, the count « Hier » shows. Its losses are « Hier »'s « Ventes perdues » to say, so the badge doesn't count them (#231). Pausing freezes everyone where they are, with their bubbles and the avatar's arm, and takes the badge down.
- **The line** (`crowd.ts`) is first come, first served: the spot in front of the counter, then one spot further left for each customer ahead. It stands on the sidewalk, left of the truck, and the lane runs in the road in front of the truck. Customers walk in from just past either edge of the street on screen, in a lane nearer the viewer so they pass in front of the line, and step into it at their spot; each moves up when the spot ahead frees. One who bought is served (the avatar reaches across the counter); one who did not looks a moment. The crowd reports everyone who reaches the counter, so the street also sees a customer who bought nothing because their last unit was botched. Then they walk off past a random edge with their mood in a bubble: happy (bought), annoyed (too expensive, or gave up on the line), sad (sold out) or neutral (only looked).
- **The people** (`people.ts`) follow the design reference's `person()` (#197), without outlines, lit from the left (see « The light »): a head with its light, a rounded torso with its gradient, two legs and two arms, the far ones in shade behind the torso, two hands always, and hair with a lit stroke or a cap with a lit edge. A hand that holds a cup or a fan bends its arm in front of the torso. Lengths are the reference's, shares of its 116 for a customer and of 130 for the owner; customers stand 106 to 126 tall. A segment shows only through an accessory, never through skin or body: the bargain hunter carries a reusable bag, the regular a folded La Presse-Citron under the arm, the chic customer wears a long coat. A customer who bought a café or a chocolat holds its cup from when the avatar serves them, never before; everyone else leaves empty-handed. A weekday adds office cues to some (a lanyard or a laptop bag); a weekend brings some families, an adult with a child, who count as one visit. Skin tones, clothes, hair (a third or so wear a cap) and heights vary at random across everyone; the shirts and the hair include the reference's, its purple shirt too. The avatar is the reference's owner behind the counter: a blue shirt and a cream apron under the side light, the far arm in shade behind it, the near one reaching across the counter, and the hair of the avatar the player picked behind the head, with a lit hairline. The mood bubble keeps its ink outline: it is a balloon over the world, like the tally, not a part of it.
- **The settings** (`crowdSettings.ts`): each speed's sampling and pace, the walking speed, how long serving, looking and the bubble last, the positions in the design, the colours, hair and heights people draw from, how their bodies take the light and the shadows at their feet (`figure`), and the card's width and spacing and the pick's margin. They only change what the world draws, so they sit here rather than in `src/content/`, an exception to CLAUDE.md rule 5 kept to this card's folder; a content card may move them into `assets.json`. How fatigue shows came later and lives in `src/content/avatarFatigue.json`, which the settings read as `fatigue`.
- **Not yet**: visits record when customers reached the counter (`servedMinute`) and who gave up on the line (`leftLine`, at `leftMinute`), but the street does not use those minutes yet. The line's length comes from how many walk on against how long the world takes to serve them, not from real waits, and one who gave up steps into the line, looks a moment and walks off annoyed. The rack shows this morning's croissants while the customers replay yesterday, so it does not empty as they buy.

## The avatar's energy

The avatar shows the owner's energy on the morning of the day in the top bar (`game.energy`), read through the engine's `fatigueOf`, as the top bar's « Énergie » gauge reads it. `fatigue.ts` holds the timings without drawing, and `avatarFatigue.json` the numbers.

- **Rested**, the avatar looks as usual, with two eyes.
- **Tired** (« Fatigué »), the arm takes longer to reach across the counter (`reachMultiplier.tired`), and the avatar yawns now and then: the mouth opens and the eyes all but close for `yawn.lengthMs`, then the next yawn comes `yawn.everyMs`, give or take `yawn.jitterMs`, after.
- **Exhausted** (« Épuisé »), the reach is slower still (`reachMultiplier.exhausted`), the avatar yawns, slumps (everything above the waist sinks, and the head sinks more and tilts at the chin) and has dark circles under the eyes.
- **The spill**: a customer for whom the owner botched a unit (`visit.botched`) leaves a coffee-brown puddle on the counter, with a drop over its front edge, near where the arm reaches. The arm reaches for them too, even when the botched unit was the last one and they leave with nothing. The spill shows for `spill.showMs`, then fades out over `spill.fadeMs`; a new one shows it in full again.
- **Time**: the reach and the spill run at the crowd's pace, and the yawns in real time, whatever the speed. Pausing freezes all three. At ×10 the arm rests and the spill clears with the street, but the yawns go on. On a closed day the avatar is gone and the spill with it.
- **Not yet**: the street replays yesterday's visits with this morning's energy, so a spill can show on a rested morning after a tired day.

## Picking a customer

The player can pick one customer at a time to hear what they think. The street keeps running; picking never pauses the game, and a customer can be picked while it is paused.

- **The card** (`customerCard.ts`) gives the customer's segment, one customer's name for it (« Habitué »), their mood as a word (« Satisfaction », « Agacement », « Déception », « Indifférence ») with a dot in the bubble's colour, and a line between guillemets from `src/content/lines.fr.json` for their segment and how their visit ended. The line is picked the first time they are picked, so picking them again shows the same one. A ring in player blue marks their feet.
- **Where it shows**: the card is part of the page, not of the world's layer, which sits under the panels: `WorldCanvas.tsx` draws it fixed on the page over the panels and the top bar, so neither ever hides it, and screen readers skip it. Each frame the street places it beside its customer's drawing (`placeCard`): to their right, its foot level with theirs, or to their left near the right edge, or above their head when neither side has room, as on a phone; always inside the page. Its height follows its text.
- **The mouse**: a press on a customer opens their card. `picker.ts` listens on the window rather than on the canvas, since the panels' boxes take the presses beside them: a press on the canvas or on what holds the world's layer (`html`, `body`, the app's root) is on the street. There the street finds the customer drawn on top under the press (`customerAt`), give or take `pickMargin` since they keep walking; elsewhere on the street, or anywhere else on the page, the card closes. A press on the card or on the picker's button does neither. The pointer shows over a customer.
- **The keyboard**: the page's first Tab stop is « Écouter un client », hidden like a skip link until it has focus, then shown at the bottom right over the panels. Enter opens the card of the next customer on screen, in the order they came, back to the first after the last. Escape closes the card, unless a text field or an open dialog has it. A status line reads each card out to screen readers, and says « Personne dans la rue pour l'instant. » on an empty street, which it also shows beside the button.
- **It closes** on a press elsewhere, on Escape, once its customer walks off the screen, when the street clears at ×10, and when the street stops.
- **The picker** (`PICKER`) is shared by the page and the street, as the store is. The street holds it while it runs; when a street stops after another has started, as React StrictMode does in development, the newer one keeps it.

The truck's inside has no room for them, and the line starts at its back. Customers walk in front of and behind them by their feet.

## The city

What the world shows follows « Ville » (#204): the city while that panel is open, the street otherwise (`worldView` in `src/ui/settings.ts`, never kept). `WorldCanvas.tsx` starts the world with `startWorld` (`worldView.ts`), which runs the street or the city and swaps them as the view changes: it stops the scene that runs, then starts the other. A pick while a scene is still starting waits for it, then swaps, so only the last view asked for shows; a scene that fails to start warns and leaves the world blank until the view changes again. When the page takes the world down while a scene is still starting, `mountWorld` destroys the app only once that scene has started and stopped, so no scene ever draws on a destroyed app; a scene whose start fails halfway takes down what it set up (`setUpOrUndo` in `teardown.ts`). The city (#209) is `city-target.svg` of the design reference: the block of the Plateau as an island of 12 by 12 squares floating in the sky, seen at an angle in 2:1 isometric (`iso2.py`, 100 by 50 a square from 720, 170). On it stand five triplexes and a café, the Caisse (the bank), the mill and its silo, the permits office, the building for rent that becomes the bakery, three grocers' shops, the truck parked in the avenue, fourteen trees, four lamps, five cones and three cars, around Parc La Fontaine and its pond.

- **The art** is SVG under `art/city/`, cut from the reference by `art/cut_city.py` (`python3 cut_city.py <reference folder>`), which checks that `iso2.py` still draws the target, that every line is in a piece or left out on purpose, and that the boxes rank the objects in the reference's paint order where they overlap; it prints the manifest's numbers, from the reference's grid. The sky is the reference's gradient stretched, its top colour above the design and its bottom colour below it, so it fills the window however the city is framed; the island (its slab and cut, sidewalks, park, roads, crosswalks and the labels « Parc La Fontaine » and « AV. MONT-ROYAL » in embedded Fredoka 600, where the reference writes the avenue in Nunito 800) is one file; every shadow the objects cast is one file too, the reference's group at its opacity with its blur, so overlaps never darken twice. Each building, tree, lamp, cone and car is its own file, with its roof details, awning or stairs. The reference has no grocers: their shops are its café, drawn by its own `box()` in empty lots east of the truck, each awning in the grocer's colour. Left out: the grain, mottle, vignette and glow (`atmosphere.ts`), the people and the trip's way, drawn in code, and the board of the building for rent, which code writes « À louer » or « Fournil ».
- **The manifest** is `city` in `src/content/assets.json`, drawn in the street's palette and `light`. A box (the island, a building, the truck, a tree, a lamp, a cone, a car) is its box on screen and `depth`, how wide its lit wall is across; its walls stand `height` − `width` / 2 tall (`boxCorners` in `iso.ts`). The box ranks it, takes the clicks and sizes its placeholder; its image is drawn at its `art`, the rectangle of the design it covers, as drawn.
