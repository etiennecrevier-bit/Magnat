The world draws the game state; it never changes it. It reads `GameState` and the last day's visits, and reports player clicks to the UI layer, which calls the engine.

- It may import the types, the engine, finance and content functions that only read, and the UI store (`useGame`, `gameStore.getState()`) to read the game. It never imports the functions that change the game (`postEntry`, `applyDecision`, `tick`, `playDay`, and the others `eslint.config.js` lists) and never calls the store's `advance` or `setState`. Its tests may, to build their fixtures.
- Engine, finance and content never import anything from here.
- Randomness and the browser are allowed here, since nothing the world does reaches the engine: a customer's walk can wobble without changing the game.
- `WorldCanvas.tsx` mounts the Pixi application in a fixed layer behind the React panels, sized to the window. While it is on the page, `WorldCanvas.css` puts the panels (`main`) on an opaque cream card with an ink outline, so no scenery runs behind their text; on a phone the card spans the window.

## The street

`WorldCanvas.tsx` starts the street with `startStreet` (`street.ts`), which loads the title font and the art, builds the scene (`scene.ts`), fits it to the window (`layout.ts`) and redraws it each time the store's game changes.

- **The manifest** is `src/content/assets.json`: every piece's id, layer, position and size in the 1440 × 900 design, its colour, and optionally an image. Layers are drawn back to front in the order listed, and pieces in each layer in the order listed. Without an image, a piece is drawn as its kind's placeholder from `placeholders.ts`: simple shapes in the palette's colours with dark outlines.
- **Real art** replaces a placeholder without code: put the file under `src/world/art/` (PNG, JPEG, WebP, AVIF or SVG) and set the piece's `image` to its path there, such as `"image": "truck.png"` or `"image": "truck/body.png"`. `art.ts` collects every file in that folder when Vite builds, and the image is stretched to the piece's size. A missing file or one that fails to load warns in the console and keeps the placeholder. `art/dummy.png` is a test image that proves the path from the folder to the screen; no piece uses it.
- **Open or closed** follows the day in the top bar (`game.day`), as `tick()` will play it: with that day's decisions, not those waiting for the next morning. A piece with `"shows": "open"` (the awning, the steam) or `"shows": "closed"` (the rolled awning, the « Fermé » sign) only shows on those days.
- **The croissant rack** shows the croissants the truck opens with that morning: those on hand plus the morning restock, or only those on hand on a closed day. Croissants keep one day, so none are on hand between days. `fill.capacity` croissants fill the rack, shown in `fill.steps` shelves; any croissant shows a shelf (`rackFill` in `truckView.ts`). The restock rule repeats the engine's, which the world may not call; `truckView.test.ts` holds the two together.
- **The sign** reads the business name from the player's profile (`game.profile.businessName`, « Le P'tit Camion » unless the player picks another) in Fredoka SemiBold (`fonts/`, SIL Open Font License), shrunk to fit.
- **Scaling** keeps the design's proportions: as large as the window allows, centred across and standing on the bottom edge. A taller window shows more sky; a wider one shows more of the rooftops, sidewalk and street, which run past the design's edges.

## The customers

`street.ts` starts the customers with `startCustomers` (`customers.ts`) and moves them on each frame at the store's speed. Each concern has its own module, and the rules are tested without Pixi.

- **What replays** (`replay.ts`): during each day, the street replays the last day played, `game.lastDay`. Its visits come up in arrival order as the world's own clock goes through the day: a day lasts `msPerDayAtNormalSpeed` at ×1, and a visit comes up once the day is as far through as its arrival is through the opening hours. The clock starts again on a new day or a new game, not when a decision changes the game. Nothing replays on a day the truck is closed: the avatar is gone, and everyone still waiting walks off without buying and without a bubble. The open day after a closed one has no visits to replay, so its street stays empty.
- **The sampling** is visual only and never changes a number. Each visit that comes up is kept with the speed's `keepChance`, so busier days and busier hours bring more people. A kept visit walks on only if the speed's gap (`minGapMs`, give or take `jitterMs`) has gone by since the last one and fewer than its `maxOnScreen` are on screen; otherwise it is dropped, never held back, so those who walk on come when they came. When the next day starts, the visits still to come are dropped. A day lasts 4 s at ×1 but a walk about 10 s, so the crowd is a stream running across days rather than one day played out.
- **The cap** gates who comes, at each speed: ×1 lets in up to 10, ×3 up to 5. Going from ×1 to ×3 takes no one off the street: those there finish their visit, faster, and no one new comes until fewer than 5 are left. At ×10, no one walks: the street clears and a badge counts the day replayed, « 87 clients servis » (visits that bought) and « 21 clients perdus » (too expensive or sold out; those who only looked count in neither). Pausing freezes everyone where they are, with their bubbles and the avatar's arm.
- **The line** (`crowd.ts`) is first come, first served: the spot in front of the counter, then one spot further left for each customer ahead. Customers walk in from just past either edge of the street on screen, in a lane nearer the viewer so they pass in front of the line, and step into it at their spot; each moves up when the spot ahead frees. One who bought is served (the avatar reaches across the counter); one who did not looks a moment. Then they walk off past a random edge with their mood in a bubble: happy (bought), annoyed (too expensive), sad (sold out) or neutral (only looked).
- **The people** (`people.ts`) are placeholders: a rounded torso, a round head and dark outlines. A segment shows only through an accessory, never through skin or body: the bargain hunter carries a reusable bag, the regular a travel mug, the chic customer wears a long coat. A weekday adds office cues to some (a lanyard or a laptop bag); a weekend brings some families, an adult with a child, who count as one visit. Skin tones, clothes and heights vary at random across everyone. The avatar stands behind the counter in a blue shirt and a cream apron, with the hair of the avatar the player picked.
- **The settings** (`crowdSettings.ts`): each speed's sampling and pace, the walking speed, how long serving, looking and the bubble last, the positions in the design, and the colours and heights people draw from. They only change what the world draws, so they sit here rather than in `src/content/`, an exception to CLAUDE.md rule 5 kept to this card's folder; a content card may move them into `assets.json`.
- **Not yet**: visits record when customers arrived, not when they were served or left, nor whether they gave up on the line (P2-03). Until then the line's length comes from how many walk on against how long the world takes to serve them, not from real waits, and no one walks away from the line. The rack shows this morning's croissants while the customers replay yesterday, so it does not empty as they buy.
