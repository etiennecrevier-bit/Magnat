Every tunable number (prices, costs, pacing, demand) lives here as JSON; no magic numbers in engine code.

`lines.fr.json` holds what a customer says when the player clicks them on the street: each segment's name for one customer, and lines for each way a visit ends (`bought`, `tooExpensive`, `soldOut`, `leftLine`, `browsed`). `lines.ts` validates it: every segment and outcome, at least 8 lines each, none longer than 120 characters or repeated within a segment. Lines name no price, since a line cannot know the real one.

`messages.fr.json` holds the messages the player receives during a game: `landlord` (from « Votre propriétaire ») when the owner's rent goes unpaid, and `exhausted` (from « Vous ») the first time in a game week the owner wakes up exhausted. Then the kinds whose sender the game names, which have `lines` and no `from` (#163): a grocer's `missedDelivery`, `contractEnded` and `contractBroken`, and an employee's `hired` and `fired`. `messages.ts` validates it: every kind, at least 5 lines each, none longer than 140 characters or repeated. Lines name no amount, and are gender-neutral, since the avatar, the grocers and the employees may be women or men.

`messageStreet.json` sets how many game days a message shows on the street from its day (`streetDays`, #232) before it folds into the « N messages » chip, which still counts it and brings it back when clicked.

`avatarFatigue.json` sets how the owner's fatigue shows on the street, which only changes what the world draws: how much slower the arm reaches when tired or exhausted, how long a yawn lasts and how often one comes, how far an exhausted avatar slumps, the colour of the dark circles under their eyes, and where the spill a botched unit leaves on the counter sits, its colour and how long it shows and fades. `avatarFatigue.ts` validates it.

`assets.json` is the world's asset manifest: where each piece of the street and the truck sits, its colour, and the image that replaces its placeholder, and under `light` how the placeholders are lit and what is drawn over the world: the sun and slate colours, the three tones, the cast, contact and ledge shadows, the materials, the grain, the painted unevenness, the vignette and the glow (see `src/world/README.md`). Its `city` is the block of the Plateau the world shows from « La ville » (#165), as `city-target.svg` of the design reference draws it (#209), in the same palette and light, with two more colours, `lamplight`, for the bakery's lit windows, and `white`, for its board: its layers, its pieces in 2:1 isometric (a box's place and size on screen and `depth`, the width of its lit wall; where its image goes, `art`; a piece on a box's wall names it in `on`), what each `shows` (the truck serving or shut, the bakery running or for rent, a grocer's contract), what a click `opens` (« Aller à… » for a place, « Fournil », « Contrats »), the place in town each building is (`place`) and the grocer whose shop it is (`grocer`), each place's way from the truck to its door (`routes`), the van's ways, stops, hours and size (`deliveries`), where the people serving, the line, the bakers and the deliverers stand, how big the street's people are drawn there (`figure`), how the dashed way and the labels look, and how the city is framed beside « Ville » (`frame`: the part of the island to keep in sight, and where the panel, the top bar and the dock are, as the stylesheets put them). The numbers come from the reference's grid, as `src/world/art/cut_city.py` prints them. `assets.ts` checks that each place has one building and each grocer one shop, that a piece on a wall names a box listed before it, and that a box is at least half as tall as wide; a place or a grocer is checked against the ids `schema.ts` knows, so each needs a way there or a stop. `assets.test.ts` holds them to `places.json` and `grocers.json`, and checks that each way ends a step in front of its place's door and goes through no other building, that the van keeps to the avenue's south lane past the truck, that the line stands on the sidewalk, and that every building a click opens stands on the island.

`foodtruck.json` holds the truck's numbers: the products (unit cost, packaging, shelf life), the truck's price and useful life, the monthly costs (permit, parking and commissary; insurance), the fuel per open day, the card payments (the share of sales paid by card and the fee), payday, the walk-ins, the service at the counter, the marketing, and the player's starting decisions and their limits. The `service` sets how long a customer takes at the counter: `baseSeconds` each, plus `perItemSeconds` for each unit ordered, sold out or not; and `maxAheadInLine`, the most customers still waiting a newcomer will stand behind. Each day type's `patienceMultiplier` scales how long its crowd waits. The `marketing` sets what the daily budget brings: awareness is the average spend of the last `awarenessDays` business days, today not counted and a closed day counting as 0, and each open day brings `extraCustomersPerSqrtCent` × √awareness (awareness in cents) walk-ins on top of the day type's crowd, before the day's noise. They come and behave like that crowd, but their segments follow the marketing's `segmentShares`.

`segments.json` holds the customer segments: how long each will wait in line (`patienceMinutes`, before the day type's multiplier), and for each product a reference price, a base purchase probability and a sensitivity.

`difficulty.json` holds the three ways to start a game (Facile, Moyen, Difficile): the cash in the bank once the truck is settled, how the truck comes to the business, and the owner's own savings (`startingPersonalCashCents`). `staff.json` holds the first employees (#158): for each of the roles `camion` (« Au camion », who serves beside the owner), `livreur` (« Livreur », who makes the grocers' deliveries) and `boulanger` (« Boulanger », who bakes at the bakery), its French `name`, the hours paid a week (`hoursPerWeek`) and the hourly wage the player may set (`hourlyWageCents`: `min`, `max`, and `start`, the wage a hire starts at, the issue's). `financing.json` holds when the two expensive ways out of a negative encaisse come (#159): `loanEvent`, the card of Mme Prudhomme's emergency loan, which comes the first morning after a close below zero of each game year of 365 days from day 0; `investorEvent`, the card of Max Duvent's offer for shares, which comes the morning she refuses, or `investorDaysAfterLoan` days after her last loan if the encaisse is still below zero, for `maxShareRounds` rounds at most; and `noSecondLoanAt`, the difficulties at which she lends once a game. She refuses too while an earlier emergency loan is still open. `appointmentEvent` (#164) is her card the morning after a trip to her Caisse, when she would lend: not yet that game year, and her loan would be above zero; it counts as her visit of the year. The terms are the cards' effects in `events.json`. `schema.ts` validates it against `events.json`: the three cards differ, and each has a weight of 0, so no draw brings it, and a choice with an effect of its kind, the loan's for the appointment. Its `bankruptcy` (#160) holds when a negative encaisse ends the game: `months`, the whole calendar months in a row with the encaisse below zero at every close, 6, and `notices`, Mme Prudhomme's letters, each the `event` of a notice in `events.json` and the `afterMonths` it comes after, 1, 3 and 5, in order, each before `months`. Tuning `months` upward can stop an ended save from loading.

`dream.json` holds « Améliorations » (#162), the ladder to climb: `personal`, the owner's own rungs in the order they are climbed, each with its French `name`, its `priceCents`, paid from the owner's personal cash, and its `bonuses`; a rung with `housing` replaces the rent with its `monthlyCents` under its `label` from the 1st it is bought, and `sells` sells back an earlier rung with housing at its price. `company`, the company's rungs, paid from Encaisse and depreciated over `usefulLifeMonths`; the one that `replacesTruck` takes the old truck back at its book value and ends its depreciation or its lease, and a `comingSoon` rung shows but is not for sale. The bonuses are `travelTime` (the best vehicle's factor on the trips of `places.json`), `serviceTime` (the counter's seconds), `line` (the most customers a newcomer finds waiting), `stock` (the most units a restock rule may name), `walkIns` (a `segment`'s walk-ins) and `closedDayRecovery` (energy a closed day gives back on top). `titles` go up with the owner's net worth, `netWorthCents` each; the last one is « Magnat ». `schema.ts` validates it: every rung of `rungIdSchema` in one ladder once, a rung coming soon after every rung for sale, `sells` naming an earlier personal rung with housing, one rung at most that replaces the truck, factors above 0 and titles going up.

`places.json` holds the places in town the owner goes to with « Aller à… » (#164): `leavesAtMinute`, the minute the owner leaves the truck, `energyCost`, the energy each trip takes, and `places`, in the order the panel lists them, each with its `id`, its French `name`, `towards` (the place after « En route vers », with its article), `does` (what the owner does there) and its `effect`: `loanAppointment` (Mme Prudhomme's appointment card the next morning), `discount` (its `share` off each unit of the next restock that buys anything), `filing` (where a card's `trip` sends the owner to file its forms, and nobody goes by hand) or `none`; then either `walkMinutes` and `visitMinutes`, or `wholeDay`. On foot, the Caisse is 25 minutes away, the mill 40 and the fournil 15; the permits office takes the whole day, the one « Changer de coin de rue » of `conesOrange` books for the next day in place of closing the truck. `schema.ts` validates it against `foodtruck.json`: every place of `placeIdSchema` listed once, minutes above 0, a share above 0 and below 1, and the owner leaving while the truck is open and back from each place, on foot, by the time it closes.

`personal.json` holds what the owner's own life costs, paid from their personal account and never by the business: `rentCents` on the 1st of each month, and `weeklyExpensesCents` (groceries, phone, transit) on payday. `schema.ts` validates it.

`energy.json` holds what tires the owner and what fatigue does, on an energy scale of 0 to 100 where a game starts at 100: each open day costs `openDayCost` plus `costPerCustomerServed` for each customer who reaches the counter, each closed day gives `closedDayRecovery` back, unless a card closed it for work (a `closed` effect with `work`), which costs `openDayCost`, and each night gives `nightRecovery`, and each payday the owner's personal cash is below zero takes `debtStress`. Below `tired.below` the owner is « Fatigué », below `exhausted.below` « Épuisé »: serving takes `serviceTimeIncrease` longer (0.2 is 20 %), and `botchedShare` of the units made are botched, written off and made again. `schema.ts` validates it.

`events.json` holds the event cards: dilemmas that come up on a morning and wait for the player's choice before the day is played. Its `pacing` sets how often: no event before game day `firstDay`, at least `minDaysBetween` days from one drawn event to the next (follow-ups do not count), then a chance of `dailyChance` each morning, the event picked by `weight` among those whose conditions hold; a weight of 0 is never drawn. An event with a `date` comes on that day whatever the gap and the chance, if its other conditions hold, unless the follow-up of a `followUpOnly` card is due that morning; another follow-up due then waits a morning. R-06 (#155) set the pacing to day 10, 14 days and 8 %: about one event every 3 to 4 weeks, a median gap of 21 days over two years. `scaling` sets how each difficulty scales what a choice gives and takes (#137): every gain (a cash or a credit sale, the owner's energy or cash going up, a change to the street above 1) by its difficulty's `gains`, every loss (a charge, the owner's energy or cash going down, a change to the street below 1) by its `losses`. Money rounds to the cent and energy to the whole point, half away from zero; a change to the street scales by its distance from 1, so ×0.65 with losses of 1.3 becomes ×0.545, and never below `lowestFactor`; stock, durations, chances and receivable terms never scale. `adaptiveDraw` lists the difficulties whose draw follows the player's standing each morning: « en difficulté » below the `behindBelowPercentile` of their difficulty and week in `percentiles.json`, or with an encaisse below `behindBelowMonthsOfFixedCosts` months of fixed costs; « en avance » above the `aheadAbovePercentile`; « dans la moyenne » otherwise. There, an event's `weightByStanding` (`behind`, `average`, `ahead`) replaces its `weight` for the standings it lists. A Défi ignores both: it plays the cards as written and draws by `weight`. Each event has a camelCase `id`, a `character` (a portrait `id`, a French `name` and `role`), a French `title`, `situation` and optional `quote`, optional `conditions` and two or three `choices`. Every condition given must hold on the morning: `fromDay`, `difficulties`, `months` (1 = January), a `date` (`month` and `dayOfMonth`), `weekdays` (ISO, 1 = Monday), `energyBelow`, `cashAboveCents` and `cashBelowCents` (Encaisse), `oncePerGame`, `maxPerGame` (it came up fewer times than this, follow-ups included), `cooldownDays`, `chosen` and `notChosen` (the player made at least one, or none, of these choices before, each an `event` and one of its `choice`s), `productOnMenu` (its restock rule buys some), `openTomorrow` (`true` when the truck must open the next day, `false` when it must stay closed: the days open hold that weekday and no change to the street, made or queued, closes it), `grocer` (that grocer of `grocers.json` may offer a contract: none of theirs runs, fewer than `maxContracts` do, and they are not away) and `followUpOnly` (it only comes as another event's follow-up). A choice has an `id`, a French `label`, the `tags` the card shows before choosing, and its `outcomes`, each with a `chance`, which the player never sees, a French `result` and its `effects`. A tag is a French text shown as it is, or an object: its `text`, shown only at the `difficulties` it lists, if any, and, when it names an `effect`, only where an effect of that `name` applies, with `{montant}` replaced by its amount at the game's difficulty, summed over the effects of that name in the first outcome holding one. A tag never writes an amount by hand, and neither does the rest of the card: the situation, the quote, a choice's label and an outcome's result write `{montant:name}` where the amount of the effects named `name` goes, at the game's difficulty, as a tag would show it. The situation and the quote read the effects of the event's choices, a label those of its choice's outcomes and a result those of its own outcome, in the first outcome holding one; so « La grosse commande » quotes the sale of « Accepter ».

The effects are `charge` (paid now, with a French `description`, to 6900 Autres charges or the expense `account` given: 5200, 6100, 6150, 6200, 6300, 6500 or 6900), `cashSale` (revenue paid now), `creditSale` (revenue now that a named `client` pays `dueInDays` later), `collectReceivable`, `delayReceivable` and `writeOff` (on what a `client` owes), `buyStock` (at the product's unit cost, or at its own `unitCostCents`, then only when the choice is made), `useStock` (oldest units first, to an `order`, a `giveaway` or a `loss`, to 5100 Pertes sur stocks), `energy` (held between 0 and 100), `personal` (the owner's own cash, with a French `label`, outside the books), `followUp` (another event `days` later, with a `chance`, 1 by default), `headline` (a La Presse-Citron headline's id), `contract` (a contract signed with a `grocer` of `grocers.json`, at their price times `priceFactor`, 1 by default), `grocerAway` (the `grocer` offers nothing for `days` days), `trip` (#164: the owner goes to a `place` of `places.json` for the day, as « Aller à… » does, a trip the player can neither drop nor trade; always on a later day), `emergencyLoan` (a loan from a French-named `lender`: enough to bring the encaisse to `monthsOfFixedCosts` months of fixed costs, `maxCents` at most, paid back monthly over `termMonths` at `annualRateBasisPoints` a year, 1800 for 18 %, with `feeShare` of it charged up front; nothing while another emergency loan is open, nor at a difficulty of `noSecondLoanAt` in `financing.json` once there was one), `shareSale` (sells `share` of the business, above 0 and below 1, to a French-named `investor` for `amountCents`, scaled like a gain, who takes that share of each quarter's profit from then on; nothing once `maxShareRounds` rounds were sold), and the changes to the street, each for `days` days from the day it starts: `walkIns` (multiplies the walk-ins of everyone, or of one `segment`, by a `factor`: 0.45 for the first week of construction, 1.8 for a festival), `appeal` (multiplies one `product`'s appeal: 0.3 for chocolat chaud in a heat wave), `willingness` (multiplies what everyone, or one `segment`, will pay: 1.1 on moving day), `unavailable` (a `product` cannot be sold) and `closed` (the truck stays closed, whatever the days open; with `work: true`, the owner works that day away from the counter, so it tires like an open day with no customer instead of resting them). Every `factor` is above 0, and the changes in effect on a day multiply each other. An effect runs when the choice is made, or later: after `inDays` days, or on the next ISO weekday `onNextWeekday` (4 is next Thursday, a week away on a Thursday), never both; a `contract`, a `grocerAway`, an `emergencyLoan` and a `shareSale` always run when the choice is made. A tag of a loan shows the most it lends, and one of a share sale its price. It applies at every difficulty, or only at the `difficulties` it lists, and may carry a `name` for its choice's tags. `schema.ts` validates it: besides each field, the event ids are unique, the choice ids unique within their event, each choice's chances sum to 1, every follow-up names an event of the file, every choice `chosen` and `notChosen` list is one of the file's, a tag showing `{montant}` names its effect, every effect a tag names is one of its choice's, of one unit (money, energy or a percentage) when the tag shows its amount, and every `{montant:name}` of a card's text names effects of one unit where it looks, at every difficulty, with no other braces in the text. So that the card fits, a situation holds at most 280 characters, a choice's label 45 and a result 200. The first card, `essai`, tries every effect for the tests; its weight of 0 keeps it out of a real game.

They are notices: a card with a single choice, « Compris », which no draw brings, at a weight of 0 with none by standing, and whose choice has one outcome and no effect; its tags may be empty. Every other card has two or three choices, each with a tag at least. Their text counts the months down and explains nothing.

The card of P4-09 (#164) ends the file, at a weight of 0: `rendezVousCaisse`, « Le rendez-vous à la Caisse », Mme Prudhomme's appointment, which the engine brings the morning after a trip to the Caisse, as `financing.json` says. « Accepter » runs the same `emergencyLoan` as `pretUrgence`, with the same tags; « Refuser » leaves the encaisse as it is, and costs some energy at Difficile.

`bankruptcy.fr.json` holds what Mme Prudhomme says on the « Faillite » screen once a game goes bankrupt: `prudhomme`, three lines at least, none twice, a game showing the one its seed picks. `bankruptcyText.ts` validates it, as `messages.ts` does `messages.fr.json`.

`defis.json` holds the Défis: short games with one goal and a medal, each 1 to 9 weeks. Each has a camelCase `id`, a French `title` and `story` (at most 400 characters), a `difficulty`, a `start` date in the first season of year 1 (`month` and `dayOfMonth`, from May 1st) and a `lengthDays`. Its `setup` replaces how a game of its difficulty opens: `cashCents`, the cash in the bank once the truck is settled, in place of the difficulty's `startingCashCents`; `prices` and `restock`, product by product, each product keeping its kind of restock rule; `daysOpen`, `ownerWeeklyPayCents` and `marketingDailyCents`; and the owner's `energy` on the first morning. `locked` lists the kinds of decision the player cannot change: `price`, `restock`, `daysOpen`, `ownerWeeklyPay` or `marketingDaily`. `modifiers` force changes to the street, each `change` a street change as an event's effect writes it without its `days`, for `days` days from the Défi's day `day`, counting from 0, made as if by a choice of the card `event`, so the world shows it. `events` force a card of `events.json` on the morning of a day, one a day at most, where the player may make only the `choices` listed. `randomEvents` lets the event cards' draws come too; follow-ups always do. The `goal` measures one `metric` over the Défi, `lowestCash` (the lowest Encaisse after any entry, in cents), `netIncome` (the bénéfice net), `sales` (the ventes), `customersServed`, `spoilageRate` (the units written off over those sold and written off) or `lowestEnergy`, and its `medals` give the least each of `or`, `argent` and `bronze` needs, or the most for `spoilageRate`, where lower is better. Its `conditions` must hold all along, or the Défi ends at once, « échoué »: `minCash` (`amountCents`), `minEnergy` (`amount`) and `maxSpoilageRate` (`rate`), each once at most. `schema.ts` validates it against `events.json`: besides each field, the ids are unique, the medals in order, the forced days within the Défi, and every forced card and choice one of `events.json`'s. The six of P3-11 (#99) are starting values that P3-14 (#102) tunes; `defis.test.ts` plays one scripted success and one scripted failure of each.

`percentiles.json` holds how 1 000 simulated owners did at each difficulty, for « Mieux que N % des propriétaires »: for each week from 1 to 104, their bénéfice net from day 0 to the close of the week's Sunday at the percentiles 5, 10, 20 … 90 and 95, in cents. It is generated, not written by hand: `npm run balance -- --percentiles` writes it with `docs/balance/phase-3.md` (see `tools/README.md`), and it goes stale whenever the engine or the content moves the numbers. `schema.ts` validates it: whole percentiles strictly between 0 and 100, going up, and each week one amount per percentile, never going down. `src/engine/percentiles.ts` reads it.

`headlines.fr.json` holds the La Presse-Citron headlines the ticker shows (`src/ui/headlines.ts` reads it): `events`, the text of each headline a card's `headline` effect queues, by its id, one for each id the cards use and no other, and `general`, the 20 everyday headlines the ticker cycles through, each one line of at most 120 characters. They mock parody institutions, name real places and are never mean about a group of people.

`report.json` sets what the « Bilan de la semaine » tells of a week (`src/engine/week.ts` reads it): at most `maxFacts` facts, the highest `weight` first, except that a bénéfice up while the encaisse is not always comes first. Each fact has its weight and when it holds: `profitUpCashDown`, a bénéfice net above 0 while the encaisse went down, or went up at least `minGapCents` less than the bénéfice, as when a big order is paid in 60 days; `unpaidRent`, the owner's rent went unpaid; `receivableDue`, a customer owes something due within `withinDays` of the Sunday; `spoilage`, the stock written off cost more than `aboveCents`; `bestWeek`, ventes above every earlier week of the game; `lostToLine`, more than `aboveVisits` visits left the line; `daysOpenInARow`, the truck opened at least `atLeast` days in a row; and `contractsReceivable`, the grocers of `grocers.json` owe more than `aboveCents`. A tie between equal weights goes in that order. `schema.ts` validates it: every fact, and whole numbers of at least 0, but at least 1 for `maxFacts` and `atLeast`.

`report.fr.json` holds what the « Bilan de la semaine » says (`src/ui/weeklyReport.ts` reads it): `facts`, the text of each fact of `report.json`, at least 2 variants each, the weeks taking them in turn, each naming every number of its fact as `{name}` (`profitUpCashDown`: `{netIncome}` and `{cashChange}`, written with its sign; `unpaidRent`: `{day}`; `receivableDue`: `{client}`, `{amount}` and `{dueDay}`; `spoilage`: `{cost}` and `{units}`; `bestWeek`: `{sales}` and `{previousBest}`; `lostToLine`: `{visits}`; `daysOpenInARow`: `{days}`; `contractsReceivable`: `{amount}` and `{grocers}`, the grocers' names joined with « et »); `headlines`, La Presse-Citron's on a week whose first fact is that one, at least 2 each, which may name the fact's numbers; `quietHeadlines`, at least 2, for a week with no fact; `advice`, « Le conseil de Mme Prudhomme », the banker's dry one-liner on a week whose first fact is that one, at least 3 each, which may name the fact's numbers and points at the thing to look at without saying why; `quietAdvice`, at least 5 and with no number, hers for a week with no fact; and `creditSaleNote`, the chart's note on the day of a sale on credit, with `{amount}`, `{client}` and `{dueDay}`. `reportText.ts` validates it: every fact, no other number and no stray brace, facts and advice of at most 160 characters and headlines of at most 120, and no line twice. The counts are always 2 or more, as `reportText.test.ts` checks against the thresholds of `report.json`, so they are written in the plural. The text describes the week and never explains a term.

`pacing.json` sets how fast the game runs: `msPerDayAtNormalSpeed` at normal speed, the `speeds` the top bar offers (0 is the pause) and `maxTicksPerFrame`, the most days one frame plays. It also sets how the « Bilan de la semaine » shows: in full, pausing the game, for the first `fullReportWeeks` weeks of a game, then as a card that slides in for `reportSlideInMs` of real time.

`cashGauge.json` sets when the top bar's cash reads low: below this many months of what every truck pays monthly.
