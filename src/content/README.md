Every tunable number (prices, costs, pacing, demand) lives here as JSON; no magic numbers in engine code.

`lines.fr.json` holds what a customer says when the player clicks them on the street: each segment's name for one customer, and lines for each way a visit ends (`bought`, `tooExpensive`, `soldOut`, `leftLine`, `browsed`). `lines.ts` validates it: every segment and outcome, at least 8 lines each, none longer than 120 characters or repeated within a segment. Lines name no price, since a line cannot know the real one.

`messages.fr.json` holds the messages the player receives during a game: `landlord` (from « Votre propriétaire ») when the owner's rent goes unpaid, and `exhausted` (from « Vous ») the first time in a game week the owner wakes up exhausted. `messages.ts` validates it: both kinds, at least 5 lines each, none longer than 140 characters or repeated. Lines name no amount, and the owner's are gender-neutral, since the avatar may be a woman or a man.

`avatarFatigue.json` sets how the owner's fatigue shows on the street, which only changes what the world draws: how much slower the arm reaches when tired or exhausted, how long a yawn lasts and how often one comes, how far an exhausted avatar slumps, the colour of the dark circles under their eyes, and where the spill a botched unit leaves on the counter sits, its colour and how long it shows and fades. `avatarFatigue.ts` validates it.

`assets.json` is the world's asset manifest: where each piece of the street and the truck sits, its colour, and the image that replaces its placeholder, and under `light` how the placeholders are lit and what is drawn over the world: the sun and slate colours, the three tones, the cast, contact and ledge shadows, the materials, the grain, the painted unevenness, the vignette and the glow (see `src/world/README.md`). `assets.ts` validates it: colours as `#RRGGBB`, opacities between 0 and 1, the two blend modes, and a shadow layer the manifest lists and no piece is in.

`foodtruck.json` holds the truck's numbers: the products (unit cost, packaging, shelf life), the truck's price and useful life, the monthly costs (permit, parking and commissary; insurance), the fuel per open day, the card payments (the share of sales paid by card and the fee), payday, the walk-ins, the service at the counter, the marketing, and the player's starting decisions and their limits. The `service` sets how long a customer takes at the counter: `baseSeconds` each, plus `perItemSeconds` for each unit ordered, sold out or not; and `maxAheadInLine`, the most customers still waiting a newcomer will stand behind. Each day type's `patienceMultiplier` scales how long its crowd waits. The `marketing` sets what the daily budget brings: awareness is the average spend of the last `awarenessDays` business days, today not counted and a closed day counting as 0, and each open day brings `extraCustomersPerSqrtCent` × √awareness (awareness in cents) walk-ins on top of the day type's crowd, before the day's noise. They come and behave like that crowd, but their segments follow the marketing's `segmentShares`.

`segments.json` holds the customer segments: how long each will wait in line (`patienceMinutes`, before the day type's multiplier), and for each product a reference price, a base purchase probability and a sensitivity.

`difficulty.json` holds the three ways to start a game (Facile, Moyen, Difficile): the cash in the bank once the truck is settled, how the truck comes to the business, and the owner's own savings (`startingPersonalCashCents`). Facile owns the truck; Moyen buys it with a startup loan, whose yearly rate is in basis points (800 is 8 %) and whose term is in months; Difficile leases it by the month. The engine opens the books and charges the truck from these. `profile.json` holds the new game screen's defaults (business name, avatar, difficulty) and the longest business name. `schema.ts` validates all three.

`personal.json` holds what the owner's own life costs, paid from their personal account and never by the business: `rentCents` on the 1st of each month, and `weeklyExpensesCents` (groceries, phone, transit) on payday. `schema.ts` validates it.

`energy.json` holds what tires the owner and what fatigue does, on an energy scale of 0 to 100 where a game starts at 100: each open day costs `openDayCost` plus `costPerCustomerServed` for each customer who reaches the counter, each closed day gives `closedDayRecovery` back and each night `nightRecovery`, and each payday the owner's personal cash is below zero takes `debtStress`. Below `tired.below` the owner is « Fatigué », below `exhausted.below` « Épuisé »: serving takes `serviceTimeIncrease` longer (0.2 is 20 %), and `botchedShare` of the units made are botched, written off and made again. `schema.ts` validates it.

`events.json` holds the event cards: dilemmas that come up on a morning and wait for the player's choice before the day is played. Its `pacing` sets how often: no event before game day `firstDay`, at least `minDaysBetween` days from one drawn event to the next (follow-ups do not count), then a chance of `dailyChance` each morning, the event picked by `weight` among those whose conditions hold; a weight of 0 is never drawn. Each event has a camelCase `id`, a `character` (a portrait `id`, a French `name` and `role`), a French `title`, `situation` and optional `quote`, optional `conditions` and two or three `choices`. Every condition given must hold on the morning: `fromDay`, `difficulties`, `months` (1 = January), a `date` (`month` and `dayOfMonth`), `weekdays` (ISO, 1 = Monday), `energyBelow`, `cashAboveCents` and `cashBelowCents` (Encaisse), `oncePerGame`, `cooldownDays`, `productOnMenu` (its restock rule buys some) and `followUpOnly` (it only comes as another event's follow-up). A choice has an `id`, a French `label`, the `tags` the card shows before choosing, written by hand, and its `outcomes`, each with a `chance`, which the player never sees, a French `result` and its `effects`.

The effects are `charge` (paid now, with a French `description`, to 6900 Autres charges or the expense `account` given: 5200, 6100, 6150, 6200, 6300, 6500 or 6900), `cashSale` (revenue paid now), `creditSale` (revenue now that a named `client` pays `dueInDays` later), `collectReceivable`, `delayReceivable` and `writeOff` (on what a `client` owes), `buyStock` (at the product's unit cost), `useStock` (oldest units first, to an `order` or a `giveaway`), `energy` (held between 0 and 100), `personal` (the owner's own cash, with a French `label`, outside the books), `followUp` (another event `days` later, with a `chance`, 1 by default) and `headline` (a La Presse-Citron headline's id). An effect runs when the choice is made, or later: after `inDays` days, or on the next ISO weekday `onNextWeekday` (4 is next Thursday, a week away on a Thursday), never both. `schema.ts` validates it: besides each field, the event ids are unique, the choice ids unique within their event, each choice's chances sum to 1 and every follow-up names an event of the file. So that the card fits, a situation holds at most 280 characters, a choice's label 45 and a result 200. The only card shipped, `essai`, tries every effect for the tests; its weight of 0 keeps it out of a real game.

`cashGauge.json` sets when the top bar's cash reads low: below this many months of what every truck pays monthly.
