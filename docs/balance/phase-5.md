Generated by `npm run balance -- --hiring --games 30 --months 12`: do not edit by hand, except under the comment at the end that says so. The numbers go stale whenever the engine or the content changes; run the command again then, and commit the file.

## What each game plays

Moyen plays seeds 1 to 30 for each role and each of the 12 candidates, to the close of the last day of month 12, or to the close that ends the game in bankruptcy (#160). Each game hires the candidate on day 0, at the wage they ask, `askingWage()` in `src/engine/staff.ts`, so none quits, and their twin plays the same seed and the same business with nobody hired. The gap is the game's Encaisse less its twin's at the end; it pays the training, the wages and what the employee's strength and weakness do, from `src/content/staff.json`. No other event card comes up.

- « Au camion » runs the « busy » truck of `docs/balance/phase-4.md`, croissants at $1.10 to buy, 110 croissants bought a day, $80.00 of marketing a day, and no contract.
- « Livreur » runs the « busy » truck of `docs/balance/phase-4.md`, croissants at $1.10 to buy, 110 croissants bought a day, $80.00 of marketing a day, and the contracts of Marché Bio-Logique and Épicerie Chez Normand, signed on day 0 and renewed.
- « Boulanger » runs the « 150 a day, 2 contracts » truck of `docs/balance/phase-4.md`, croissants at $1.10 to buy, its 150 croissants a day made at a bakery opened on day 0 with its oven leased, the owner baking them in the twin, $80.00 of marketing a day, and the contracts of Marché Bio-Logique and Épicerie Chez Normand, signed on day 0 and renewed.

Each table shows, by candidate, the median over the seeds: the gap, the days absent with work to do, the units botched at the truck, the owner's and the employee's, and the croissants the baker threw away; « Bankrupt » counts the games that ended in bankruptcy, their twins' in parentheses.

## What the run says (5-03, #255)

- **Both targets of step 6 are met**, after one change to the content. With the starting values, every trait asking a $1.50 premium and taking a $1.00 discount, every candidate asked the same $0.50 above their role's start, and the three weak in fiabilité (Nadia, Thierry, Olivier) lost money in every role: absent 22 days a year, their bakery buys the truck's 150 croissants at $1.10 and the grocers' at full price, without the Bio-Logique premium. Fiabilité acts in every role, so it now moves the asking wage most (`askingPremiumCents` 250, `askingDiscountCents` 300 in `src/content/staff.json`): the reliable ask $1.00 more than the others, the unreliable $2.00 less, and Olivier, the weakest, comes out about $500 ahead of the owner baking alone.
- **The baker is the hire that pays.** Baking costs the owner 8 energy a morning, so a twin whose owner bakes is tired and loses customers; every baker gains $500 to $2,500 in a year. Minutie shows most there: the careful bakers throw away half the croissants of the others, and Chloé and Kevin, weak in it, gain the least after Olivier.
- **An employee at the truck never pays within a year**, as in phase 4: from $19,000 to $29,000 behind (median), and as many as 27 games in 30 end in bankruptcy: Maude Leblanc, asking $19.00 and weak in accueil, the regulars' trait, is $28,852 behind and bankrupts 27; the next worst bankrupt 10 (Samuel Lavoie) and 9 (Mathieu Pelletier), and the three weak in fiabilité none. That is at $560 to $665 a week against the customers a second server keeps in line on the busy truck. The cheapest candidates come first: there, the asking wage outweighs the traits. Rapidité and accueil move the line and the regulars, but by less than the wage.
- **A deliverer only costs while the owner is at work**: the owner would have delivered, so the gap is the wage and the training, $8,745 at $16.50 an hour, and more when the deliverer is absent on a Monday and the delivery is missed. A deliverer earns their keep when the owner is away on a Monday, which this run does not play.

For a later card: an employee at the truck or a deliverer pays only when the owner can be away, on a trip or at a second truck; the hiring run plays the owner at work every day.
