# Balance report: phase 4

Generated by `npm run balance -- --bakery --games 30 --months 24`: do not edit by hand, except under the comment at the end that says so. The numbers go stale whenever the engine or the content changes; run the command again then, and commit the file.

## What each game plays

Each difficulty plays seeds 1 to 30 with each truck strategy below. A game opens the bakery on day 0, Monday, May 1st, year 1, with its oven leased or bought, makes its croissants there from that day on, and plays 24 months, to the close of the last day of month 24. Its twin plays the same seed and the same truck, buying its croissants: each morning, the bakery makes as many as the twin buys. No event card comes up. Every difficulty plays every price set, whatever `src/content/difficulty.json` holds; the oven, the move and the energy of a morning of baking are `src/content/bakery.json`'s. The truck opens 6 days a week, as the starting decisions have it, and keeps their other prices and the owner's pay.

## What the run says (P4-02, #157)

The bakery ships in P4-02 without the contracts of P4-01 (#156), as Étienne decided: no Marché Bio-Logique at « artisanal » prices, no contract volume. So this run measures the bakery against the truck's own croissants only, and `src/content/difficulty.json` gives every difficulty the mid prices until P4-01 picks a set for each.

- **Made costs less than bought** at every set: $0.45 to $0.65 of ingredients against $1.10 from the supplier.
- **Under 80 a day it never pays back**, at any set, oven or difficulty: the starting truck sells about 50 croissants an open day and no game of it pays back within 24 months.
- **Above 150 a day it does not win yet.** In the books, the bakery breaks even at 130 croissants made an open day at best (low prices, bought oven) and at 209 with the shipped mid prices and a leased oven; making 150 a day, only the low prices with a bought oven come out ahead, by $334 a month. In play, no game pays back: the street buys about 92 croissants a day at most, the rest spoils, and the trucks that bake sell fewer.
- **Baking wears the owner out.** A morning of baking takes 8 energy on top of the open day, so a week of six open days takes about 118 where the nights and Monday off give back 80, and the owner closes at 11 or 12 energy instead of 71 to 77: exhausted, they botch units and lose customers in line, and sell 3 to 9 fewer croissants a day, besides the drinks. That costs more than the bakery saves at every set: making 150 a day, the bakery game ends its 24 months $33,262 behind its twin at the low prices with a leased oven, and $81,812 behind at the high prices with a bought one. P4-03's baker takes the morning off the owner.
- **The difficulty changes nothing here.** It sets the truck's charges and the books a game opens with, which a game and its twin share, so the three tables are the same. Each difficulty's set has to come from what differs between them once the contracts come in: the cash to carry the bakery until it pays back.

For P4-01: the targets of #157 (paying back after 4 months in Facile, 6 in Moyen and 9 in Difficile with 150 croissants a day and two contracts) need the contracts' volume and the Bio-Logique premium, and likely P4-03's baker, or a lighter `bakingEnergyCost` in `src/content/bakery.json`. Run `npm run balance -- --bakery` again once they are in, and choose each difficulty's set from it.
